    Each mound's area is the total change from the first to the last reading in the years you picked. Every mound uses the same scale, so a mound twice the area is twice the climb. The rings inside count the share of periods that rose.

    Data: FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. Series NASDAQCOM, A939RC0Q052SBEA, MEHOINUSA646N, CUSR0000SEHA, CPIFABSL; inflation from CPIAUCSL; wages from AHETPI; grocery prices from BLS average prices (APU0000708111, APU0000709112, APU0000702111, APU0000703112). Data pulled .

    Each move is the change from the previous reading at that series' own frequency. "After inflation" divides every series by the overall Consumer Price Index for the same period, so a rise means it beat inflation; for rent and groceries it means they got pricier relative to everything else. Stocks are the Nasdaq Composite averaged to months, because FRED only publishes the last 10 years of the S&P 500; Nasdaq leans toward tech and swings harder.

    Hours of work use the average hourly wage of production and nonsupervisory workers (about 80% of private-sector jobs). Rent in dollars starts from the Census 2000 median gross rent of $602 a month and grows with the rent CPI, which covers all tenants and trails new-lease rents by about a year.
